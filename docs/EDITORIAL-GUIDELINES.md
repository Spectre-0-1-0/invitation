# Editorial Guidelines - College Memory Archive

To maintain the quality, emotional impact, and premium feel of the archive, please follow these guidelines when adding content.

## 1. Writing Style
- **Tone:** Nostalgic, warm, personal, and authentic.
- **Perspective:** First-person plural ("We", "Our") for collective memories; first-person singular ("I", "My") for senior quotes and messages.
- **Language:** Avoid corporate speak (e.g., "Synergy", "Operational Excellence"). Use evocative imagery (e.g., "Library sunrises", "The roar of the stadium").

## 2. Senior Profiles
- **Quotes:** Max 150 characters. Should reflect the student's personality or a defining lesson from college.
- **Nicknames:** Encouraged if they were widely used by the batch.
- **Achievements:** Focus on the "why" as much as the "what" (e.g., "Led the team to victory after a 3-year drought").

## 3. Memories & Photos
- **Image Requirements:** High resolution (Min 1080p). Natural lighting preferred.
- **Descriptions:** Every photo needs a context. Who was there? What happened just before the shutter clicked?
- **Categories:**
    - `candid`: Unstaged, real-life moments.
    - `classroom`: Academic focus, studio vibes, lectures.
    - `farewell`: Graduation week, final goodbyes.
    - `trip`: Outings, field trips, study abroad.

## 4. Messages
- **Categories:**
    - `thank-you`: Gratitude towards mentors or friends.
    - `funny`: Inside jokes and lighthearted roasts.
    - `appreciation`: General praise for the batch or university.
    - `farewell`: Parting words and future wishes.

## 5. Metadata Tagging
- Always link seniors to memories using their unique `id`.
- Use consistent date formats (`YYYY-MM-DD`).
- Ensure every timeline event has an `importance` level (`major` vs `minor`) to maintain visual rhythm.
