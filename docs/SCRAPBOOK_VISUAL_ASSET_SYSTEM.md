# Scrapbook Visual Asset System: The Materials of Memory

This document defines the physical ingredients that form the visual language of the College Memory Archive. Every asset described here must feel like it was collected, saved, and placed by hand over the course of a four-year journey.

---

## PART 1: VISUAL MATERIAL PHILOSOPHY

### The Tactile Truth
A scrapbook is not a digital interface; it is a physical container. The materials are the medium of the story.

*   **Paper:** The foundation. It holds the weight of the years.
*   **Tape & Ink:** The evidence of effort. They show that someone wanted these memories to stay.
*   **Found Objects:** The "traces" of life. They prove that the student was actually there—in the lab, in the canteen, in the exam hall.
*   **Photos:** The window into the past. They vary in quality and stock depending on their origin.

**Emotional Purpose:** To move the user from "viewing a site" to "holding a legacy." Every material should evoke the specific tactile nostalgia of student life.

---

## PART 2: PAPER SYSTEM

The paper is the stage for the memory. It varies by context and chapter.

### 1. Found Academic Materials (The Traces)
*   **Lab Record Fragments:** Grid paper, often slightly yellowed or stained with chemical splashes. Used for technical milestones.
*   **Assignment Sheets:** Lined paper with "Problem Set" headers. Often crumpled and smoothed back out.
*   **Blue/Pink Exam Books:** The distinctive texture of internal test paper. Used for "The Struggle" and "The All-Nighter."
*   **Viva Notes:** Small, torn fragments of paper used for frantic last-minute preparation.
*   **Attendance Slips:** Thin, inexpensive paper used to represent the daily grind.
*   **Library Issue Slips:** Catalogued and stamped. Used for reflective or research-heavy moments.

### 2. Creative & Journal Papers
*   **Fresh Notebook Paper:** Clean, white, or off-white. Dominant in Year 1.
*   **Graph Paper:** Representing the engineering mindset.
*   **Colored Construction Paper:** Used for "Festival Week" or "Club Recruitment" highlights.
*   **Torn Notebook Edges:** Showing the "perforated" look of a page ripped in haste.

**Emotional Mapping:**
*   **Year 1:** Crisp, organized, high-quality notebook pages.
*   **Year 2-3:** A mix of torn-out lab sheets and messy assignment scraps.
*   **Year 4:** Reflective, higher-weight journal paper mixed with "Placement Drive" notices.

---

## PART 3: PHOTO SYSTEM

Photos communicate their origin through their material stock.

*   **Polaroids (The Intimate):** Thick white borders, slightly faded colors, matte finish. Used for personal, casual "in-the-dorm" memories. High frequency in Years 1-2.
*   **Professional Event Photos (The Public):** High-gloss, sharp, clean edges. Used for Graduation, Prize Distributions, and Major Festivals.
*   **Phone Prints (The Common):** Standard 4x6 feel, slightly "digital" grain but printed on paper. Used for everyday moments and group selfies.
*   **Photo Strips (The Social):** Narrow vertical strips from photo booths. Used for "The Crew" and "The Best Friends" pages.
*   **Clipped Snapshots:** Photos with one or more edges cut (sometimes poorly) to fit into a cluster.
*   **Printed Screenshots:** Chat snippets or social media memes printed on standard computer paper and taped in.

## PART 4: HANDWRITING SYSTEM

The scrapbook is a collective effort. We define 10-20 recurring "personas" to maintain character consistency.

### Personality Archetypes
1.  **The Neat Student (Architect):** Perfect script, blue ink, straight lines. Starts with careful notes (Year 1) and ends with reflective wisdom (Year 4).
2.  **The Chaotic Friend (Speedster):** Scrawled, hurried, black ink. Content is mostly "OMG" and "Can't believe we did this."
3.  **The Artist (Creative):** Stylized lettering, uses markers and colored ink. Found near doodles and festival pages.
4.  **The Class Clown (Prankster):** Large, bold, messy print. Mostly jokes, nicknames, and arrows pointing at goofy faces.
5.  **The Emotional Writer (Nostalgic):** Cursive, soft ink, long-form entries. Found in Year 4 and the Final Chapter.
6.  **The Lab Partner (Technical):** Mix of numbers and text. Blocky, utilitarian. Found on lab records and project pages.

**Maturity Rule:** Identities remain the same (same handwriting style), but the tone shifts from "excited/shallow" to "deeply nostalgic/reflective" as the years progress.

---

## PART 5: DOODLE SYSTEM

Doodles are the "unspoken" language of the classroom.

*   **Arrows:** Used to link two memories or point at a specific person in a group photo.
*   **Stars & Hearts:** Emotional "punctuation" for achievements or friendships.
*   **Reaction Sketches:** Small "stick-figure" versions of students reacting to events (e.g., a fainted stick figure on an Exam page).
*   **Inside Joke Icons:** Small recurring symbols (a specific coffee brand, a lab instrument, a "Room 302" logo).
*   **Margin Scribbles:** Patterns drawn while bored in a lecture (geometric shapes, swirls).

**Frequency:** Increases dramatically in Year 2 and Year 3 (The Chaos Chapters).

---

## PART 6: TAPE & ATTACHMENT SYSTEM

How an object is held reveals the care (or lack thereof) of the collector.

*   **Transparent Tape:** The "utility" choice. Often has visible bubbles or a slightly yellowed edge.
*   **Washi Tape:** Decorative, semi-transparent patterns. Used by "The Artist" or for "Festival" pages.
*   **Colored Electrical Tape:** Representing the engineering lab influence. Used to hold "Lab Disaster" memories.
*   **Paper Clips:** Used to hold a "cluster" of related notes or a stack of photos.
*   **Staples:** Permanent, industrial. Used for "Workshop Certificates" or "Placement Offers."
*   **Folded Corners (Dog-ears):** Used on pages that have "hidden" content or long stories.
*   **Envelopes:** Taped onto the page to hold "secret" notes or small physical tokens like festival wristbands.

---

## PART 7: STICKER SYSTEM

Stickers must have a "source" in the college world.

*   **Academic Stickers:** "Passed," "Certified," or department-specific symbols.
*   **Club Logos:** Specific stickers for the Coding Club, Music Society, etc.
*   **Event Badges:** Fragments of stickers used for entry to festivals or hackathons.
*   **Reaction Stickers:** "Mood" stickers used by students to comment on a memory (e.g., a "Burning the Midnight Oil" sticker).
*   **Department Crests:** The "Official" stickers of the university.

**Rule:** Avoid generic emoji. Use assets that feel like they were bought at a campus shop or earned at an event.

---

## PART 8: WEAR & AGING SYSTEM (SUBTRACTIVE STORYTELLING)

Damage is not decoration; it is history.

*   **Coffee Stains:** Found on "The All-Nighter" or "Exam Week" pages.
*   **Ink Smudges:** Where someone's hand dragged across fresh writing.
*   **Tape Residue:** Where a photo was clearly removed or moved to a different page.
*   **Torn Corners:** Showing that the book has been stuffed into a backpack or handled repeatedly.
*   **Faded Writing:** On the oldest pages (Year 1), showing the passage of time.
*   **Fold Marks:** Where a large sheet (like a timetable) was folded to fit the book.
*   **Scratched-out Text:** Representing a change of heart or an edited memory.

---

## PART 9: CHAPTER VISUAL IDENTITIES (THE 4-YEAR ACCUMULATION)

### Year 1: The Fresh Start
*   **Personality:** Clean, organized, hopeful.
*   **Materials:** Crisp notebook paper, careful handwriting, few overlaps.

### Year 2: The Deepening
*   **Personality:** Active, experimental.
*   **Materials:** More "Found" lab materials, first signs of "wear," more doodles.

### Year 3: The Chaos
*   **Personality:** Overloaded, energetic, "The Peak."
*   **Materials:** Maximum density, overlapping photos, neon markers, heavy sticker use, multiple handwriting styles on one page.

### Year 4: The Richest
*   **Personality:** Dense, meaningful, heavy.
*   **Materials:** The "thickest" feel. Placement drive notices mixed with emotional notes. Worn edges are prominent.

### The Last Page: The Echo
*   **Personality:** Quiet, minimalist, emotional.
*   **Materials:** High-weight journal paper, single high-quality photo, fading ink, lots of breathing room.

---

## PART 10: THE AUTHENTICITY TEST (THE PROVENANCE RULE)

For every visual asset, the designer/developer must ask:

1.  **Origin:** How did this object end up in the scrapbook? (e.g., "Saved from the Canteen," "Torn from a lab notebook").
2.  **Placement:** Who placed it here? (e.g., "The Neat Student" or "The Chaotic Friend").
3.  **Time:** Does the "wear" match the story? (e.g., is the coffee stain on a page about study, or a page about sleep?).
4.  **Believability:** Would a real engineering student actually save this?

**If the answer to any of these is "I don't know," the asset is rejected.**
