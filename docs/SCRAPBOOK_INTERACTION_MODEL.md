# The Digital Scrapbook: Interaction Model

This document defines how people experience, uncover, and honor memories within the College Memory Archive. It focuses on the human and emotional mechanics of interaction, treating the digital interface as a physical, tactile object.

---

## PART 1: INTERACTION PHILOSOPHY

### The Living Memory vs. The Static Archive
Interacting with the scrapbook should feel like **Discovering**, not **Browsing**.

*   **Discovery** is active, curious, and rewarding. It requires attention.
*   **Browsing** is passive, linear, and utilitarian. It requires efficiency.

The scrapbook rejects the "SaaS" model of interaction. There are no "users" here, only **Memory Collectors**.

### Core Pillars:
1.  **Tactile Intuition:** Interactions should mimic physical movements (lifting, flipping, smoothing, pointing).
2.  **Reward for Attention:** The more time you spend looking, the more you should see.
3.  **Human Imperfection:** Interactions should not be "pixel-perfect." A note might lift slightly unevenly; a photo might overlap a bit too much.
4.  **No Utility-First Thinking:** We do not optimize for speed; we optimize for depth of feeling.

---

## PART 2: MEMORY INTERACTIONS

### 1. Encountering (Seeing & Finding)
*   **Emotional Goal:** Curiosity and Surprise.
*   **Physical Metaphor:** Catching a glimpse of something familiar in a box of old papers.
*   **Usage:** Seeing a blurred edge of a photo or a recognizable color in a margin.

### 2. Engaging (Opening & Inspecting)
*   **Emotional Goal:** Intimacy and Detail.
*   **Physical Metaphor:** Picking up a photograph to look at the back, or smoothing out a crumpled note.
*   **Usage:** Moving closer to an element to read the fine print or see the texture of a memory.

### 3. Honoring (Revisiting, Sharing, Remembering)
*   **Emotional Goal:** Connection and Legacy.
*   **Physical Metaphor:** Handing a physical photo to a friend or pointing at a face in a crowd.
*   **Usage:** "Passing the memory" to someone else ("Look at this, remember this?") rather than "Sharing to Feed."

---

## PART 3: PHOTO INTERACTIONS

### Pinned, Taped, and Stacked
Photos are the weight of the scrapbook. They should feel like physical artifacts.

*   **Taped Photos:** Feel temporary and spontaneous. Engaging with them might reveal a "sticky" resistance.
*   **Stacked Photos:** Encourage exploration. You have to "move" the top one to see the treasure beneath.
*   **Overlapping Photos:** Create depth. They hide and reveal fragments, forcing the eye to piece the story together.

### The Experience of "Looking Closer"
When someone focuses on a photo, it shouldn't just "zoom." It should feel like they are bringing it into the light. The textures (grain, paper weight, fingerprints) become clearer, making the memory feel "held."

---

## PART 4: NOTE INTERACTIONS

Notes provide the "voice" of the archive. They are the annotations of a life lived.

*   **Sticky Notes:** Ephemeral "shout-outs." They are bright, messy, and feel like they were slapped on in a moment of excitement.
*   **Handwritten Notes:** The narrative heart. They require reading and reflection. They are found in margins or tucked between photos.
*   **Quotes:** The emphasis. Large, bold, and unmissable. They represent the shared language of the class.

**The Role of Discovery:** Notes are often "uncovered." They provide the "why" behind the "what" of the photographs.

---

## PART 5: DISCOVERY SYSTEM (A HIERARCHY OF ATTENTION)

Discovery is the reward for the user's presence.

1.  **Common Discoveries:** Large photos, main titles, obvious stories. (Visible to all).
2.  **Emotional Discoveries:** A poignant quote, a heartfelt message, a "Then vs Now" moment. (Requires a full page turn).
3.  **Hidden Discoveries:** A note behind a photo, a doodle in the extreme margin, a small joke "under" a tape fragment. (Requires active curiosity).
4.  **Rare/One-Time Discoveries:** An emotional handwritten note tucked into a farewell page. The rarity comes from its placement in a quiet corner of the book. (Requires deep attention).

---

## PART 6: MEMORY REVEAL SYSTEM

Memories reveal themselves at the speed of understanding.

*   **Immediate Memories:** The "Face Value." You see the graduation cap, the party, the club logo.
*   **Earned Memories:** Memories found because you looked closer or stayed longer. You notice the "Room 302" scribble or the tiny "I was here" note.
*   **Contextual (Delayed) Memories:** Meaning that emerges through the journey. A joke on Page 10 only becomes funny when you see the event on Page 50. The scrapbook "rewards" the memory of the user.

---

## PART 7: USER JOURNEY STATES

| State | Interaction Focus | Emotional Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arrival** | Opening the cover, tactile invitation. | Anticipation & Identity |
| **Curiosity** | Moving elements, seeing fragments. | Engagement & Wonder |
| **Exploration** | Deep dives into clusters and chaos. | Discovery & Joy |
| **Recognition** | Finding familiar faces and places. | Connection & Belonging |
| **Reflection** | Reading long-form notes, quiet spaces. | Bittersweet Nostalgia |
| **Closure** | The final page, closing the book. | Peace & Legacy |

---

## PART 8: ANTI-INTERACTIONS (DIGITAL POLLUTANTS)

To preserve the scrapbook's soul, we must avoid these "modern" pitfalls:

*   **The Database Mindset:** No search bars, no complex filtering, no "sort by date." Navigate by memory, not by query.
*   **The Infinite Scroll:** A scrapbook has pages. It has a physical limit. "Endless clicking" is the enemy of reflection.
*   **Gamification:** No badges, no "unlocks," no XP. We reward curiosity, not completion.
*   **Surprise without Purpose:** No random "pop-ups." Every reveal must be grounded in the physical metaphor (e.g., lifting a flap).
*   **Mechanical Uniformity:** No perfectly centered elements. No "Standard UI" buttons. Everything must feel placed by a human hand.

## PART 9: THE PERFECT SCRAPBOOK SESSION (A NARRATIVE)

A senior sits down on the evening of graduation. They open the digital cover—it has a heavy, textured feel that suggests years of use.

They start in **Chapter 1**. The pages are sparse. They see their old student ID and laugh at how young they looked. They "lift" a taped-on orientation map to find a scribbled note about a first-day coffee disaster.

By **Chapter 3**, the energy shifts. The pages are chaotic. They spend ten minutes in a "Memory Cluster" of a festival week, "moving" photos around to see the faces hidden in the back of the crowd. They find a "Rare Discovery"—a tiny doodle in the margin that references a joke from their Freshman dorm. They don't "share it to a feed"; they take a mental snapshot and think, "I need to show this to Arjun later."

In **Chapter 6**, the chaos settles. They read the "Sticky Note Wall." The varied handwriting makes it feel like their friends are in the room. They pause at a "Handwritten Note" from a favorite professor. They feel the weight of the four years.

Finally, they reach the **Last Page**. A single, quiet photo of a sunset over the campus library. There is no "Back to Home" button. Only the tactile sensation of the back cover. They close the book. They sit in the silence for a moment, feeling the closure of a chapter.

The scrapbook has done its job. It wasn't a website they visited; it was a memory they revisited.
